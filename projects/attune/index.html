The design question

How can a week of small experiences become something worth revisiting?

Attune is designed for young adults in college and early careers. The aim is to make space for challenge, restoration, creativity, and connection through activity discovery and reflection.

02 / Giving reflection a form

From cut glass
to a weekly memory.

In summer 2026, I spent more than 20 hours sourcing, cutting, and assembling glass into a lotus mosaic. That process inspired Attune’s weekly history: individual experiences forming a larger composition.

A personal making practice became a way to think about remembering.

The proposed interaction

One activity.
One piece of the week.

Each logged activity becomes an element-colored tile. The interaction study lets you choose an element, log an activity, inspect its tile, and undo changes. It demonstrates one part of the broader History and logging experience shown above.

Tile size has no quantitative meaning. It does not represent time, effort, frequency, or importance.

Working interaction study

Try a week taking shape.

Open the full playground ↗

Choose an element, name a moment, and watch it join the week. Or load a sample mosaic to explore. Each new activity splits the largest piece; Undo restores the previous composition. This is a focused interaction study, not the full mobile app.

Your entries stay in this browser. Tile size does not represent effort or achievement. The mosaic uses the current Figma colors and a fixed Frosted finish; its appearance controls are intentionally removed.

Promising appeal.

Responses also associated tile size with time, frequency, or importance. That tension needs testing: the visual should invite reflection without implying a score. These are concept reactions, not evidence of successful use or improved wellbeing.

03 / Helping people find an activity

A clearer view
of personal interests.

The survey compared swiping through suggestions with selecting interests from a visible set. Interest tiles were preferred in 20 of 35 responses, informing the move toward showing interests together rather than one at a time.

Early lo-fi alternatives

Sequential or side by side?

The card deck asks people to consider one interest at a time. The grid keeps options visible together, with selections that can be compared before continuing.

These wireframes test the direction of Explore. They are early concept work, not the final visual design.

From preference to iteration.

I replaced the sequential selection with visible interest tiles so people could see their choices together. Subsequent user testing helped refine the Explore page, with minimal improvement feedback in its final testing round.

The chart records the earlier concept survey. It is not a measured usability improvement or a final product outcome.

Where the project is now

Refining the concept
through use.

Attune is an in-progress capstone. My partner and I have moved from concept explorations into higher-fidelity History, logging, and Explore designs, informed by user feedback.

The next questions are practical: can people interpret the mosaic without reading value into tile size, find older activities as it fills, and switch to a list when that is more useful?

The screens and interaction study on this page capture the current iteration. They are not final, and longer-term product outcomes have not been established.
